the overflow five

Our five core values

Our mission: to create moments and spaces that fill people beyond what's in their cup.

Hospitality

Before someone experiences our coffee, they experience how we make them feel.

See the person, not the order.

Craft

Every drink and every interaction is a chance to serve someone well. Thoughtful details communicate dignity.

The last drink gets the same care as the first.

Connection

More than a place to grab a drink: a space where conversations flourish and neighbors become community.

Invite guests into the fun.

Delight

Thoughtful details remind people that goodness still exists and ordinary moments are worth celebrating.

Look for the Overflow Moment.

Overflow

Everyone leaves a little more encouraged than when they arrived, with joy overflowing into our neighborhood.

Fill each other up first.

with guests

Notice, listen, act

Notice the person, not just the order. Make one genuine connection attempt with every guest and listen for natural chances to make their visit special. Never forced, never an interrogation.

  1. Notice

    Nobody walks in unnoticed. On bar and can't help yet? "Hi! We'll be right with you!"

  2. Listen

    Pick up on what's going on: a first visit, a celebration, a hard day, a familiar face.

  3. Act

    Turn that opening into something that makes their visit special.

What we expect with every guest

  • Greet them right away. Every guest, every time, even if you're in the middle of a drink.
  • Learn names. Make it a point to know them and use them.
  • Give thoughtful suggestions. Ask what they usually like, then recommend something that fits.
  • Invite them in. Bring guests into the conversation behind the bar.
  • Walk it out. Bring for-here drinks and pastries to their table.
  • Say goodbye too. Thank people on their way out, not just when they walk in.
  • Fix it, no questions. If a drink isn't right, remake it happily.
  • Know the menu. Be able to explain any drink, our two drips and our teas.
  • Make kids feel welcome. Talk to them, not just their parents.

Handling tough moments

  • Drink not right? Remake it, no questions and no charge.
  • Unhappy guest? Stay calm, listen, apologize and offer a fix. Don't take it personally.
  • Refunds: if a remake won't fix it, refund the item in Square. Don't argue. Text Kynzey about anything bigger or repeat issues.
  • Rude or unsafe guest: you can ask anyone who is harassing, threatening or making others unsafe to leave. Keep it calm and direct: "I need to ask you to leave."
  • If they won't leave or you feel in danger, call 911. Never physically remove anyone.
  • Theft: don't chase or confront. Note what happened and tell Kynzey.
  • Call or text Kynzey if someone is hurt, equipment breaks, the till is off, or you're just not sure.

Guest FAQs

  • Wi-Fi: network Overflow Guest, password fillyourcup
  • Dogs: well-behaved dogs are welcome inside, but we can ask them to leave if needed. Always offer a free pup cup!
When it gets busy

Embrace the chaos. Have fun, stay calm.

A long line just means people want to be here. Take it one drink at a time, help each other out, and keep the energy light. Guests can feel it either way.

      behind the bar

      Standards, safety and slow times

      Milk & drink standards

      • Steam to 140–155°F, aiming for about 150°F. Hotter starts to taste flat and burnt.
      • Add air early, before the milk passes about 100°F, then spin it into silky microfoam.
      • Kids' temp: 120–130°F for steamers and kids' hot chocolate.
      • Extra hot: up to about 165°F. Never boil the milk.
      • Latte art on every drink.
      • Use a thermometer until you can feel the right temp by hand.

      When it's slow

      • Restock sauces
      • Make simple syrup
      • Make cold foam
      • Clean the bathroom
      • Restock teas
      • Restock drip beans

      Safety

      • The first aid kit and fire extinguisher are in the closet.
      • In an emergency, call 911 first, then Kynzey.

      the practical stuff

      Shifts, pay, and policies

      Your shift

      • Arrive on time or early. Be ready to jump in on bar when your shift starts, not walking in at that time.
      • Clock in with Square.
      • Opening and closing checklists are right here in the handbook.
      • Clean as you go. A clean bar says "we care" too.

      Scheduling and time off

      • Schedules are posted in Square 2 weeks in advance.
      • Swaps: find a teammate, then get Amelia's approval.
      • Calling out: text Kynzey as far in advance as possible. The night before is much better than the morning of.
      • Request time off at least 2–4 weeks in advance.

      Pay and tips

      • Payday is every other Friday, through Square.
      • Tips are split with whoever is working that day.

      Breaks, drinks and snacks

      • A paid 10-minute rest break for every 4 hours you work.
      • A 30-minute meal break when your shift is 6 hours or longer.
      • Shift drinks: 1–2 drinks a day.
      • Eat and drink outside of the bar, and store your drinks outside the work area.

      Food safety

      • Get your Oregon food handler card within 30 days of starting, and email it to Kynzey.
      • Wash hands often, and label and date everything.

      Dress and phones

      • Wear something casual and comfortable that you can move and work in.
      • Keep it work-appropriate: nothing too revealing, and no clothing with offensive words or graphics.
      • Closed-toe shoes, always. Safety first behind the bar.
      • Your phone stays in your pocket.

      Perks

      • 50% off drinks on days you're not working.
      • 20% off everything else, working or not: pastries, coffee beans, merchandise, etc.
      • Try every drink on the menu during training.
      • Discounts are for you only, not family or friends.

      rituals

      Huddles, traditions, and time together

      The 60-second huddle

      Before every shift we pick one hospitality challenge:

      • Make somebody laugh.
      • Learn three people's names.
      • Notice someone before they have to ask for help.
      • Find one person we can surprise.

      Bonus round: best guest interaction of the day gets their lunch bought.

      Weird little traditions

      • First drink of the day: everybody cheers.
      • Beautiful latte art: "Wait, don't take it yet. Look at this."
      • Your birthday: a ridiculous little crown behind the bar.
      • A regular's 100th visit: Polaroid.
      • Your first perfect rosetta: photo.
      • Big life news: we celebrate.
      • Your last shift: your drink is the secret-menu special.

      Time together

      • Every month: team breakfast.
      • Every so often: family dinner after closing.
      • Once a year: the Overflow field trip to 3–4 cafés.

      What we celebrate

      Craft matters, and we'll celebrate it. But how people feel is what makes someone great here.

      "We were slammed, and somehow everyone at the bar still felt included."

      cup day

      Fill Someone Else's Cup Day

      Overflow covers the first guest's drink, up to $8. They can put up to $8 toward the person behind them, or simply receive. Whenever the chain stops, Overflow puts in another $8 and starts it again.

      The most important rule

      No pressure. Ever. Sometimes you pour, sometimes you receive. Both are the point.

      What to say

      "Someone before you filled your cup! If you'd like to fill the next person's, you can put up to $8 toward theirs. Totally optional."

      Yes: "Amazing! You just filled someone's cup." No: "Of course! Enjoy it. That's what it's for."

      At the register

      • Covers the next drink up to $8. Leftover change doesn't carry forward.
      • Ring it with the [Fill a Cup] button.
      • Guests still tip on their own.
      • Gifts over $8 go in the bank and restart the chain first.
